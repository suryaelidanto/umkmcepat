# Sparse-data design research: source review and citation map

This appendix records the 100 sources used in [`sparse-data-design-synthesis.md`](sparse-data-design-synthesis.md). Each source was fetched through Firecrawl and reviewed for usable evidence, scope, and limits. The source IDs are the citations used in the synthesis.

- Manifest: `.firecrawl/sparse-data-research/source-manifest.tsv`
- Captures: 100 expected, 100 present, 0 missing
- Extracted Markdown: 3,372,255 bytes
- Categories: foundations 16, design systems 27, accessibility 15, philosophy 11, content and trust 10, AI workflows 21
- Corrected captures: S051, S077, S078, S079, S093, and S099 now point to substantive pages or full papers rather than error pages or landing pages.
- A source marked **E** reports research or an empirical observation. **P** is a standard, official technical reference, or primary paper. **G** is practice guidance from a design system, platform, or research team. **I** is an index, essay, or editorial example used for hypotheses rather than universal evidence.

"Verified" means that the source capture was present and substantive at collection time. It does not mean that every recommendation is current forever, applies to every audience, or has the same evidentiary strength.

## Citation decisions

The synthesis gives the most weight to:

- W3C and related accessibility standards for hard requirements. [S044-S048]
- Official government and design-system guidance for structured design practice. [S017-S058]
- Empirical studies for hypotheses about hierarchy, reading, credibility, and evaluation, with age and sample limits stated. [S001-S016] [S064] [S070] [S078] [S098] [S099]
- Vendor guidance for AI workflow design, context management, transparency, and evaluation, with internal-study limits stated. [S080-S097]
- Essays and examples for philosophy and visual direction, never as proof of usability or conversion. [S059-S069] [S074] [S076]

No source was used to justify inventing business facts, assets, prices, locations, testimonials, awards, ratings, certifications, guarantees, or CTA targets. Those boundaries come from the project's product and trust requirements and remain stricter than any generic design or frontend prompt.
